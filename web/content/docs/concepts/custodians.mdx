---
title: Conviction custodians
description: The reason on record for every position you hold — so "why do I own this?" always has an answer.
---

**A conviction custodian is your recorded reason for holding a position.** Every name in
your book is anchored to one of three things:

- **a [source](/docs/concepts/sources)** — you hold it because a manager you follow
  holds it,
- **your own thesis** — you hold it because _you_ decided to, on your own reasoning,
- **the system** — it's there mechanically (an index fund, a placeholder, a sweep).

That's the whole idea. It sounds administrative until you try to answer the question
without it.

## The problem it solves

Portfolios accumulate. A position enters for a good reason and then the reason quietly
leaves: the manager who convinced you sold out, the thesis you had in 2023 stopped being
true, or you honestly can't remember why it's there. The position stays, because nothing
ever prompts you to revisit it.

Anchoring makes the reason explicit at the moment you hold it, so later — when you're
deciding what to sell to fund something new — you're choosing between _stated reasons_
rather than between tickers.

## Orphans: positions with no reason left

An **orphan** is a position you hold that no source's conviction backs anymore. The
clearest case: a manager you copy exits a name, you don't see the disclosure, and you
keep holding a position that its originator abandoned.

Two things about how orphans are reported:

- **The report never acts.** It flags; you rule. It will not sell, will not auto-anchor a
  position to a source it guessed at, and will not decide something is a mistake.
- **Right after you import a book, everything is an orphan** — nothing is anchored yet.
  That's a setup step, not an alarm, and the app says so rather than showing you a red
  wall of nothing.

You can also declare what's **out of scope**: cash is ballast, not a conviction, and
some holdings are deliberately off-strategy. Scope is set in your construction rules, and
excluded holdings are named and counted rather than silently dropped — you can always see
what wasn't examined.

## Copy fidelity: are you actually following them?

If you set out to follow a manager, **copy fidelity** measures how well you actually are:
how much of your book overlaps theirs, and which positions account for the biggest gaps.

It's the difference between believing you're running someone's strategy and knowing it.
Fidelity is measured against what you could realistically have bought — your exclusions
and untradeable names are applied first — because an infidelity you're structurally
incapable of correcting isn't a measurement, it's noise.

## Anchoring a whole book at once

Anchoring 80 positions by hand would be exactly the kind of machinery this product is
supposed to spare you. So there are two paths, and both stay: click a dropdown on any row
to anchor one position in context, or ask your
[connected AI assistant](/docs/mcp-assistant) to propose anchors for the whole book with
its reasoning, review the proposal in conversation, and let it apply the batch once you
approve.

The AI executes an anchoring **you state**. It doesn't decide what you believe.
