---
title: Reconcile
description: Comparing what you actually hold against what you meant to hold — and the trade list that closes the gap.
---

**Reconciling is comparing one real account's holdings against a saved
[target](/docs/concepts/target)**, position by position, to see exactly where reality has
drifted from your plan.

Every portfolio drifts. Prices move, you buy something on a Tuesday impulse, a manager
you follow rotates out of a name and you don't notice for two months. Drift isn't a
failure — it's physics. Not being able to _see_ it is the problem.

## What the comparison shows

For each name, reconciling shows what your account holds today, what the target says it
should hold, and the difference between the two — in weight and in dollars. Positions the
target doesn't include at all show up too, which is often the more useful half.

The comparison runs against **one account at a time**, deliberately. A target expressed
as percentages only means something relative to a specific pot of money; averaging seven
accounts together would produce a plan you can't actually execute in any of them.

## The trade plan

The output is a **trade plan** — the buys and sells that would close the gap, ordered by
size, with the cash implication stated plainly ("$15k of buys, $15k of sells, $15 net").

It is a **proposal, and only a proposal.** You can export it to CSV or print it and run
the trades yourself at your broker. Nothing in this product places an order because you
looked at a trade plan. (The one exception, the
[automated execution module](/docs/alpaca-portfolio-manager), is a separate paid feature
that starts on a simulated account and needs two deliberate actions from you before it
can reach real money.)

Small differences are filtered out by the minimum-trade rule you set in your
[construction rules](/docs/concepts/blend#construction-rules-the-constraints-you-set-once),
so the plan is a list you'd actually work through — not fifty $4 adjustments.

## What else lives on this page
