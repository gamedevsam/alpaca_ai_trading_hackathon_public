This product has its own vocabulary, and you shouldn't have to guess at any of it. Each
term below is defined in one line here, and has its own page with the longer story: the
problem it solves, what it does for you, and where it shows up in the app.

If you only read one thing, read this: **you own the portfolio, the system does the
work of keeping it honest, and nothing happens without you approving it.**

## The vocabulary, at a glance

### Manager

An investor whose portfolio you follow — a fund manager, a YouTube investor, a friend
whose picks you respect. You record what they hold; the system does the rest.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/managers)

### Source

Any reference portfolio that can feed your plan: a manager you track, your own written
strategy, an AI sleeve. Sources are _inputs_ — they are never your brokerage accounts.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/sources)

### Snapshot

One dated record of what a source held on a given day. Snapshots are append-only: a new
disclosure adds to the history, it never overwrites it.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/sources#snapshots-are-dated-and-append-only)

### Provenance

Where a number came from — a filing, an interview, a video, your own inference — carried
with the number for the rest of its life.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/sources#provenance-where-a-number-came-from)

### Freshness

How old a source's data is, computed and shown wherever that source is used, so a plan
built on a six-month-old disclosure says so out loud.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/sources#freshness-how-old-is-this)

### Trust weight

How much a given source counts toward your plan, expressed as a percentage. Your
judgment, applied consistently.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/blend#trust-weights-how-much-each-source-counts)

### Blend

Your recipe: the trust weight on each source, plus the rules the system applies when it
combines them. Saved, versioned, and reversible.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/blend)

### Construction rules

The constraints applied while building your plan — what to exclude and where its money
goes instead, position caps, how much to keep in cash, what you can't trade.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/blend#construction-rules-the-constraints-you-set-once)

### Target

The portfolio your blend produces: a list of holdings and weights, each line traceable
back to the sources and rules that put it there.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/target)

### Effective bets

How many genuinely independent bets your book makes, after names that move together are
counted as one. Usually a smaller — and more honest — number than your position count.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/target#effective-bets-how-many-bets-are-you-really-making)

### Reconcile

Comparing your target against one real account's holdings to see exactly where you've
drifted.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/reconcile)

### Trade plan

The buy and sell list that would close that drift. It is a proposal, exportable to CSV
or print. Nothing here places an order.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/reconcile#the-trade-plan)

### Conviction custodian

Your recorded reason for holding a position: the source you copied it from, your own
thesis, or the system. Answers "why do I own this?" for every line.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/custodians)

### Orphan

A position you hold that no longer has a reason attached — the name a manager quietly
exited while you kept holding.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/custodians#orphans-positions-with-no-reason-left)

### Copy fidelity

How faithfully your real book tracks a source you meant to follow, and the largest gaps
between the two.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/custodians#copy-fidelity-are-you-actually-following-them)

### AI Council

A panel of AI portfolio managers with distinct, editable investing philosophies. They
research, debate, and recommend — grounded in what you actually hold.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/ai-council)

### Prediction

A claim a manager (or you) actually made, recorded word-for-word, kept separate from the
falsifiable condition that will grade it.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/predictions)

### Scorecard

The record of how those claims turned out, per manager and per kind of claim — with
counts instead of percentages until the sample is big enough to mean anything.
[Full definition →](/docs/concepts/predictions#scorecards-that-refuse-to-flatter)

### MCP

The open standard that lets the AI assistant you already use connect to your portfolio
directly, so you can work from a chat window instead of a form.
[Full definition →](/docs/mcp-assistant)

## Two words about what none of these do

Every concept above is a **read, a record, or a proposal**. None of them place a trade,
move money, or change your holdings on their own. The one part of the product that can
touch a brokerage account —
[automated execution](/docs/alpaca-portfolio-manager) — starts on a simulated account and
requires two separate deliberate actions from you before it can go anywhere near real
money.
